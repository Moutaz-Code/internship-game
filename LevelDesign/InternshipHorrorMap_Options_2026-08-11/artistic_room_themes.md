# Artistic Room And Environmental Theme Pass

Status: Artistic level-design proposal. It does not authorize implementation or final art production.

## Unifying Direction

The facility should feel like a once-practical nuclear site that has been kept alive through contractor repairs long after its intended service life. The visual language is **institutional nuclear brutalism under improvised maintenance**:

- massive concrete and lead shielding provide the architectural base;
- replaceable steel machinery, pipe banks, casks, consoles, and contractor equipment provide room identity;
- the palette begins cooler and cleaner near the entrance, becomes warmer and heavier around containment, shifts to wet cyan around coolant, and ends in ochre/red industrial warning language around fuel and waste;
- deterioration is communicated through patched systems, abandoned work, condensation, corrosion, and interrupted procedures rather than gore;
- every room has one hero silhouette, one dominant material family, and one recognizable ambient sound family.

The Owl should feel like it inhabits the facility rather than decorating it. Spaces provide believable perches, occluded crossings, machinery noise, and evidence of recent passage, but no room becomes a theatrical monster arena.

## Room Themes

### Contractor Decontamination And Dispatch

- Diegetic function: arrival, suit preparation, contract briefing, rod intake, and emergency return.
- Visual theme: modular contractor equipment inserted into older concrete architecture.
- Materials/colors: faded blue-grey panels, off-white polymer, brushed steel, restrained cyan status strips.
- Hero silhouette: the intake rack beneath a large contract-status board.
- Horror character: the last orderly and comparatively human space; the deeper facility is visible but not explained.
- Gameplay support: immediate objective comprehension, clean carried-object approach, unmistakable return landmark.

### Radiological Assay Lab

- Diegetic function: receiving, sampling, and verifying suspect fuel components.
- Visual theme: compact clinical workstations inside a hardened shell.
- Materials/colors: pale green work surfaces, stainless steel, yellow radiation accents, thick observation glass.
- Hero silhouette: paired assay benches or sample hoods.
- Horror character: abandoned procedures, unfinished logs, and equipment left in a safe state too quickly.
- Gameplay support: a readable objective pocket with two ways around the benches rather than a terminal trap.

### Reactor Control And Instrumentation Gallery

- Diegetic function: SCRAM controls, reactor monitoring, coolant status, and facility alarms.
- Visual theme: late-20th-century institutional control technology maintained with newer contractor modules.
- Materials/colors: warm amber displays, dark control glass, beige/grey consoles, red emergency controls used sparingly.
- Hero silhouette: a long console bank facing the reactor side of the facility.
- Horror character: instruments continue reporting a reactor that nobody is operating.
- Gameplay support: readable room edge, reliable console-shaped cover, and strong destination identity without sign dependence.

### Inaccessible Reactor Core

- Diegetic function: sealed reactor vessel and biological shielding.
- Visual theme: monumental, untouchable, and visually heavier than every human-scale room.
- Materials/colors: near-black steel, dark graphite concrete, restrained cold blue from deep inside the vessel.
- Hero silhouette: the circular reactor mass itself.
- Horror character: constant low-frequency presence; occasional structural sounds suggest scale, not a scripted attack.
- Gameplay support: map-scale landmark and sightline terminator. It never becomes playable space.

### Lead-Glass Observation Gallery

- Diegetic function: visual inspection of the sealed core without access.
- Visual theme: an extremely thick wall-wide observation assembly with layered frames and service shutters.
- Materials/colors: blue-green lead glass, black frame steel, faded cream inspection markings.
- Hero silhouette: one continuous curved observation band following the existing reactor boundary.
- Horror character: players can see more than they can reach, while reflections and parallax complicate certainty about movement.
- Gameplay support: orientation, anticipation, and a dependable reactor-side landmark; never a doorway.

### Containment Shield Gallery

- Diegetic function: shield staging, control access, and protected movement around the reactor.
- Visual theme: monumental exposed floor broken by four massive shielding fins.
- Materials/colors: warm grey concrete, dark lead seams, sparse orange inspection numbers.
- Hero silhouette: large shield slabs with visibly credible height and thickness.
- Horror character: long quiet gaps make crossing decisions feel consequential.
- Gameplay support: dependable full cover, readable exposure commitments, and several quiet-relocation choices.

### Primary Coolant Pump Hall

- Diegetic function: coolant circulation, pressure control, and water chemistry monitoring.
- Visual theme: wet mechanical density organized around one circular pump/basin assembly.
- Materials/colors: desaturated cyan, oxidized steel, dark rubber, pale mineral deposits.
- Hero silhouette: the central circular coolant assembly surrounded by low pump clusters.
- Horror character: drips, pipe knocks, and pump cycling mask uncertain movement without becoming random noise.
- Gameplay support: short sight breaks, machinery distractions, and a legible loop around the central equipment.

### Air Filtration And Scrubber Service

- Diegetic function: contaminated-air treatment and emergency pressure management.
- Visual theme: vertical filter cartridges and service ducts compressed into a narrow industrial room.
- Materials/colors: muted chemical green, zinc-grey housings, amber maintenance panels.
- Hero silhouette: a bank of tall filter vessels.
- Horror character: breathing machinery creates the uncomfortable impression of a living facility without adding literal biological decoration.
- Gameplay support: acoustic concealment, localized machinery distraction, and cover that does not block the entire connector.

### Spent Fuel Transfer And Waste Handling

- Diegetic function: cask inspection, shielded transfer, damaged-fuel isolation, and waste preparation.
- Visual theme: ordered lanes beside a clearly marked hot boundary.
- Materials/colors: ochre steel, rust-brown wear, black/yellow handling marks, restrained red hot-zone accents.
- Hero silhouette: parallel cask rows with visible lateral crosscuts.
- Horror character: scratched numbering and displaced casks imply that something interrupted an otherwise strict procedure.
- Gameplay support: lane commitments, lateral escapes, carried-rod clearance, and no universal safe aisle.

### Tool Crib And Maintenance Stores

- Diegetic function: replacement parts, portable metal objects, contractor tools, and reset-safe storage.
- Visual theme: dense inventory kept behind an orderly cage or locker system.
- Materials/colors: blue-grey lockers, bare galvanized shelves, orange tool cases.
- Hero silhouette: paired tall lockers or a caged parts counter.
- Horror character: missing tools and open compartments suggest recent unseen activity.
- Gameplay support: reliable source of portable distractions without scattering decorative clutter through every room.

### Extraction Identities

- Point A — Vehicle Airlock: broad, operational, and immediately legible; best fit for the primary contractor exit.
- Point B — Maintenance Lift: tighter, industrial, and mechanically vulnerable; strongest as a risky alternate exit.
- Point C — Emergency Control Egress: institutional and protected, but remote from the entrance; strongest for a final cross-facility escape experiment.

These are thematic identities for the existing extraction rooms. They do not select or relocate the active extraction point.

## Horror And Art Rules

- Use deterioration gradients, interrupted work, and machine behavior instead of gore.
- Keep the entrance relatively orderly so deeper disorder has contrast.
- Give each major room one dominant silhouette; do not create identity through prop density.
- Keep interactables cleaner and more deliberately colored than ambient machinery.
- Let coolant, containment, and fuel have different acoustic signatures as well as different colors.
- Make every full-cover mass visually thick enough to promise protection.
- Preserve readable floor boundaries and portals even when lighting becomes darker.
- Use the Owl's implied passage—shifted tools, recent scrape marks, interrupted alarms—sparingly and without inventing a second objective system.
- Avoid tiny lore text as a navigation dependency. Room identity must survive when signs cannot be read.
- Treat final materials, lighting, bespoke decals, and environmental storytelling assets as later production scope after the spatial layout is validated.
