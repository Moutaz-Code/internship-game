     - Designed the code architecture so the desktop controller can later be replaced by an

     successfully deposited rods.

     explicit success/failure states, pause controls, and deterministic restart behavior.
     - Implemented desktop movement features including sprinting, jumping, coyote time,
     input buffering, acceleration, braking, air momentum, crouching, and leaning.

     after dropping objects, supporting deliberate throwing, and adding forgiving but
     occlusion-safe pickup targeting.

     disengagement, stimulus response, and NavMesh navigation behavior.
     - Implemented Prototype 2’s readable route-adaptation system. The Owl now gathers

     becomes suspicious, adapts its route choices, and weakens its prediction when the
     player varies tactics.
     - Added safeguards against omniscient AI, including perception-gated knowledge,
     inferred-evidence rate limiting, last-credible-position tracking, confidence decay,
     capped patrol bias, and forced patrol variety.

     Knowledge gained:

     - Learned how to structure a Unity prototype so desktop simulation, XR input, and
     future physical hardware can share gameplay systems without becoming tightly
     coupled.
     - Learned that complete physical state restoration is essential when transferring
     objects between scripted carrying and normal Rigidbody simulation.
     - Learned how frame rate can affect seemingly unrelated gameplay behavior. The
     standalone WASD issue was caused by CharacterController movement thresholds
     becoming significant at high refresh rates rather than by missing keyboard input.
     - Gained a stronger understanding of fair adaptive AI: an enemy should act only on
     information it could plausibly observe or infer, and adaptation should create pressure
     without making a player’s strategy unusable.
     - Learned the importance of separating AI perception, evidence, decision-making,
     state transitions, and navigation so each part remains testable and replaceable.
     - Learned how tactical variety can act as counterplay by reducing an AI system’s
     confidence in its previous prediction.
     - Learned that developer telemetry and baseline toggles are important when
     evaluating whether intelligent behavior is genuinely functioning or merely appearing
     random.
     - Learned to use deterministic seeds to combine replay variation with reproducible
     debugging and automated testing.
     - Learned that authored variation can provide replayability without the cost and
     unpredictability of fully procedural level generation.
     - Gained experience designing facilities around multiple viable routes, sight breaks,
     landmarks, optional shortcuts, and non-jump critical paths.
     - Learned that greybox presentation still requires careful attention to doorway
     clearance, collider placement, room identity, lighting, text scale, and overlapping
     surfaces.
     - Learned that player-facing terminology should be simple and consistent; for example,
     “scan” communicated the rod-identification action more clearly than the internal
     engineering term “assay.”
     - Learned that feedback devices require unique meanings. Heat confirms live rods,
     vibration communicates Owl proximity, and wind communicates extraction progress.
     - Gained an understanding of fault-tolerant hardware architecture, where disconnected
     or failed physical devices must not interrupt the game loop.
     - Learned that desktop first-person camera effects cannot automatically be transferred
     into VR because headset comfort, physical posture, and camera ownership require
     separate testing.
     - Learned the difference between automated verification and human design validation.
     Tests can prove logic, connectivity, reset behavior, and regressions, but they cannot
     prove comprehension, comfort, tension, pacing, or perceived fairness.
     - Improved my understanding of iterative prototyping: each pass should answer a
     focused question, preserve measurable evidence, and avoid expanding into unrelated
     gameplay systems.
