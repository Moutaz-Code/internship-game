•   Configuring Cisco Catalyst 2950 series switch and its ports with PuTTY: This task was an
    immersive learning venture that took place in the sixth week of our internship. The process started
    by powering the switch on through connection to a power plug socket. Then, various connections
    were carefully made, including: first, powering the switch; second, creating a connection through
    the console cable; and finally, fusing a USB-to-serial converter connected to the laptop. By firmly
    pressing the mode button for 15 seconds, the switch was thoroughly reset, providing a blank canvas
    for configuration. Next, the device manager was launched from the laptop and a careful inspection
    of the ports section was made to locate the relevant switch, which was designated as "Prolific USB-
    to Serial Comm port (COM4)." The next steps were starting the PuTTy program, choosing the
    serial option, and then choosing COM4 as the Serial Line as seen in Figure 23. The "open"
    command was then clicked to start PuTTy terminal, for configuration of the switch. Setting up the
    switch's hostname and accompanying password was the first step in navigating the setup
    environment. The development of distinctive VLANs was the next step, each matched with various
    capabilities — IT, HR, Wi-Fi, Staff, Student, and Faculty — was a crucial component of this
    process to smooth out traffic. Furthermore, port configuration was the next step since the VLANs
    were now ready to be assigned. Although the switch had 24 ports in total, the configuration process
    carefully concentrated on a certain subset. The process started by entering the configuration
    terminal, followed by the targeted selection of a port utilizing the "int" command, accompanied by
    the appropriate port number. Once this alignment was established, the specific VLAN assignment
    was performed through the "switchport access" command, ensuring seamless integration with the
    respective VLANs. Lastly, configuring VLAN300 also occurred when we defined the port range,
    containing specific ports for configuration, using the "int range fa0/5, fa0/10" command. Then, we
    advanced by executing the appropriate VLAN assignment using the "switchport access vlan300"
    command as seen in Figure 24. To ensure configurations were properly saved, we used the “wr
    mem” command after every step.
