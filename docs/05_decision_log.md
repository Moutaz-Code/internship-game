| D-024 | 2026-07-20 | The Owl knocks the player unconscious and implies predation without directly depicting feeding or graphic harm. | This preserves menace while remaining appropriate for potential child players and venue operation. | Active | Revisit after age-rating, stakeholder, and playtest feedback. |
| D-025 | 2026-07-20 | Preserve banked rod deposits after capture; scatter carried objective items back into authored world positions. | A capture should cost progress without erasing completed work or ending a paid session immediately. | Active | Revisit if recovery becomes trivial or scattered items become frustrating to relocate. |

| D-027 | 2026-07-20 | Recover the player through one of roughly five authored recovery zones rather than true random teleportation. | Authored zones prevent geometry errors, unfair placements, and capture-as-fast-travel exploits while retaining uncertainty. | Active | Revisit after route and recovery-time testing. |

| D-029 | 2026-07-20 | Center every contract on a highly intelligent entity that learns and adapts with the player. | Mutual adaptation, similar to the desired Alien: Isolation tension, is a core product idea alongside physical haptics. | D-030 | 2026-07-20 | Present contracts as employer assignments while selecting gameplay contracts randomly from eligible content. | This preserves contractor fiction and supports quick arcade variety without requiring a narrative mission-selection layer. | Active product direction | Revisit when more than one contract exists. |

| D-032 | 2026-07-20 | Build contracts as curated packages with selective compatibility tags. | Curated combinations protect theme, balance, haptic meaning, and QA while allowing deliberate reuse. | Active product direction | Revisit only after at least two contracts reveal real compatibility needs. |
| D-033 | 2026-07-20 | Treat contracts as narratively isolated arcade scenarios. | Short venue sessions benefit from immediate comprehension and should not depend on previous runs or story knowledge. | Active | Revisit if a lightweight framing narrative materially improves retention. |

| D-035 | 2026-07-20 | Escalate Owl capture through equipment reboot on first recovery, a short nest escape and stronger adaptation on second recovery, and terminal failure on third capture. | Each capture becomes more memorable and severe without immediately ending a paid session. | Active for Prototype 1 testing | Tune interaction length and define the exact second-stage adaptation response. |
| D-036 | 2026-07-20 | Shorten post-recovery protection to approximately 20-30 seconds during the final five minutes. | Late-run pressure should remain high without allowing immediate recapture. | Active for Prototype 1 testing | Tune against extraction pacing and recovery-zone distance. |
| D-037 | 2026-07-20 | Use encounter-severity tiers: near miss is sensory, physical contact disrupts an item or equipment, and full capture triggers relocation and escalation. | Tiering makes consequences readable and prevents every close call from applying an incoherent pile of penalties. | Active for Prototype 1 testing | Revisit after players can accurately describe what happened. |
| D-038 | 2026-07-21 | Reset entity memory at the end of every run; use suspicion after one observed use and meaningful adaptation after two. | Run-only memory suits isolated arcade sessions, avoids profile dependencies, and gives players a readable window before counterplay changes. | Active | Revisit if within-run learning is too fast to notice or too slow for a 15-20 minute session. |
| D-039 | 2026-07-21 | Start with route repetition, hiding repetition, and distraction/stun reliance as the first three adaptation signals. | D-040 | 2026-07-21 | Communicate adaptation through changed search animation, patrol routes, and responses to prior behavior, with no player-facing adaptation notification. | Players should discover intelligence through the entity's behavior rather than debug-like exposition. | Active | Operator-only telemetry remains allowed for testing. |
| D-041 | 2026-07-21 | Allow entities to bluff, test predictions, fake disengagement, and check unexpected routes. | Uncertainty and deliberate-looking behavior are central to perceived intelligence. | Active product direction | Each bluff must preserve readable counterplay and cannot use hidden player location. |
| D-042 | 2026-07-21 | Make tactical variety weaken entity confidence; repeated tactics strengthen it. | Cycling tactics creates mutual adaptation and discourages one dominant strategy without hard-disabling player tools. | Active | Revisit after telemetry shows whether variety is meaningfully rewarded. |
| D-043 | 2026-07-21 | After the second Owl capture, strengthen the adaptations already learned rather than applying only generic aggression. | The escalation should feel personal to the player's behavior rather than like a simple difficulty multiplier. | Active for Prototype 1 testing | Exact parameters remain pending the first route-adaptation test. |
| D-044 | 2026-07-21 | Use an invisible pacing director that may create believable entity back-off windows. | A 20-minute VR session needs intensity control so intelligence does not become constant exhaustion or unavoidable pressure. | Active | The director cannot falsify perception, teleport the entity, or erase consequences without believable behavior. |
