# Vision Brief

Last updated: 2026-07-20

## Executive Summary

Pixoul should prototype a short-session VR sci-fi extraction horror game that uses venue-specific hardware, haptics, and physical effects to create an experience customers cannot replicate at home.

The approved direction is a polished MLP by 2026-08-28: one 15-20 minute single-player contract featuring extraction gameplay, replayable variation, an adaptive threat, and at least one meaningful haptic or physical integration. The wider game direction supports a roster of different contract scenarios.

## Design Direction

Genre: VR near-future sci-fi extraction horror with roguelite-inspired 15-20 minute runs.

Player count:

- Vertical slice: 1 player.
- Future direction: expandable to 2-4 players if hardware, operations, and timeline allow.

Game structure:

- Players receive dangerous contractor missions from an employer.
- Gameplay selects a contract randomly from the currently eligible curated packages.
- A contract combines a site, physical objective, intelligent entity, secondary dangers, run conditions, and extraction rule.
- Combinations should be curated for readability, theme, balance, hardware mappings, and QA rather than promising unrestricted mix-and-match content.

Current vertical-slice setting:

- Abandoned, restricted, industrial, experimental, or dystopian facility.
- Fixed start and extraction zones.
- Procedural-feeling room or sublevel arrangement between them, preferably built from authored modules and variable states.

Persistent fantasy:

The player is a capable contractor taking dangerous paid jobs, not a helpless victim or military hero. The exact work, location, and entity can change between isolated arcade contracts. The consistent identity comes from the contractor role, visual language, and the rhythm of entering, doing the work, adapting to an intelligent hunter, and leaving in one piece. Pixoul's hardware makes threats and mission states something the player can physically feel.

Current proof contract:

> Enter a post-apocalyptic nuclear facility, identify and transport three live fuel rods, survive the Owl Thing, complete extraction, and leave alive.

## Reference Titles

- Alien: Isolation: stalker threat, stealth tension, survival horror pacing.
- Lethal Company: short extraction loop, replayability, panic under pressure.
- R.E.P.O.: physical item handling, extraction chaos, co-op horror energy.
- Rain World: hostile ecosystem feeling, emergent creature behavior, player vulnerability.
- Half-Life: Alyx: VR physicality, object interaction, pacing, atmosphere.

## What The Prototype Must Prove

- A first-time player understands the objective within 2 minutes.
- The core loop has tension without requiring complicated explanation.
- Run variation is noticeable across repeat plays.
- A haptic or physical effect improves the experience rather than feeling like a novelty.
- The demo can run reliably without developer intervention.

## Contract-Level Core Loop

1. Player enters facility.
2. Player receives a clear paid contract.
3. Player plans a route through authored rooms with variable states.
4. Player performs physical objective work such as identifying, transporting, repairing, stabilizing, or activating.
5. An intelligent entity studies and pressures the player while environmental hazards add secondary suspense and complications.
6. Player varies spatial hiding, sight breaks, environmental misdirection, route control, and temporary defense as the entity adapts.
7. Capture creates a serious setback that is initially recoverable rather than an automatic run loss.
8. Immediate mistakes consume time or increase danger; score is secondary to completing the contract and surviving.
9. Player completes the extraction holdout and leaves before the 20-minute deadline.

For the current slice, the physical work is assaying and transporting fuel rods, and the primary entity is the Owl Thing.

## Current Non-Goals

These are not rejected permanently, but they should not be assumed for the Aug 28 MLP:

- Full procedural level generation.
- Multiplayer.
- Persistent character progression.
- Complex inventory economy.
- Fully machine-learned enemy AI.
- Large enemy roster.
- Multiple biomes.
- Full customer identity or NFC profile integration.
- Multiple hardware integrations unless the first one is stable.
- Multiple finished contract scenarios for the August MLP.
- A promise that every site, objective, and entity can combine with every other module.
- Long-form narrative continuity between contract scenarios.
- Pre-run loadout choice or prep economy before the core loop is validated.
- Score as the primary objective or main success condition.

## Quality Attributes

- Comfort: no required rapid spinning, sprint panic, or forced artificial motion.
- Readability: objectives and interactables must be visible in low light.
- Safety: haptic and physical effects should be opt-in, calibrated, and non-startling beyond acceptable entertainment use.
- Reliability: one operator should be able to run the demo repeatedly.
- Replayability: variation should be visible in objectives, hazards, routes, or threat behavior.
- Immersion: physical feedback should map clearly to in-world events.
- Age-appropriate horror: target roughly age 12 and above through suspense, implication, and restrained consequences rather than explicit gore or feeding.

## Success Metrics

- 15-20 minute complete session length.
- Objective understood within 2 minutes.
- At least 3 run variables change between sessions.
- At least 1 haptic or physical effect meaningfully affects player experience.
- Internal testers report desire to replay.
- Prototype can be demoed reliably without developer intervention.
